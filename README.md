# Lab Homepage of Sander Nieuwenhuis | Leiden University

Lab homepage, publications, and CV of Sander Nieuwenhuis, cognitive neuroscientist at Leiden University (Netherlands).

This repository provides an overview of research on **cognitive neuroscience**, with a focus on how fluctuations in **arousal**, **attention**, and **cognitive control** influence brain function, perception, and decision-making.

# The Cognitive Neuroscience of Arousal, Attention, and Decision-Making

## Research focus

We investigate how moment-to-moment changes in brain state shape cognition and behavior. A central goal is to understand why human performance varies over time, even in stable environments.

Our work combines experimental, physiological, and computational approaches to study the neural mechanisms underlying these fluctuations.

---

## Main research themes

- The role of the **locus coeruleus–norepinephrine (LC-NE) system** in human cognition  
- Effects of **phasic and tonic arousal** on perception, attention, and decision-making  
- Moment-to-moment fluctuations in **attention and cognitive control**  

---

## Methods

We use a multidisciplinary approach, including:

- Behavioral experiments  
- Electroencephalography (EEG)  
- Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI)  
- Pharmacological manipulations  
- Pupillometry (pupil-linked arousal measurements)  
- Patient studies  
- Computational modeling of decision-making (e.g., drift diffusion models)

---

## Computational modeling

A key component of our research is computational modeling of cognitive processes, including evidence accumulation models such as the **drift diffusion model**, which links neural activity, attention, and decision-making behavior.
